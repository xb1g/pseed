## Problem Statement

PassionSeed has 23 shipped features across 6 categories, zero payment integration in the codebase, and 871 students in a running hackathon program. Two products have taken real money — TechSeed (100฿, online, multi-week) and BizSeed (~2,990฿, one day, onsite at Chula). Neither appears in the features doc.

The stated question was which order to sequence the free surface: radar → plan → pathlab, or radar → pathlab → community. That is a product-order question. The blocking question is a pricing question: **what is a transformed cohort worth to a Thai family?** Nobody has asked.

The company is currently shifting weight onto radar + PathLab because they are "easier to scale." Neither has ever been priced.

## Demand Evidence

Real, in the founder's own words:

- **TechSeed** — "we collect 100baht for each student for online tech camp. from 0." One paid batch. Many free batches. Multi-week format.
- **BizSeed** — "hosting onsite event at chula uni, targeting inter kids who wants certs, one day, 2990฿ i think." Medium-hard to fill.
- **The retention split** — "kids are just wanting certs to fill ports, like techseed. but they are just richer, they dont stuck around much, techseed alums are changed bc it's weeks, so there are bonds, and they grew so much."
- **The segment split** — "it's split, some are hopeless, some are already good."
- **871 participants** in the hackathon program (per the Phase 2 design doc, 2026-04-25).

What this is not: nobody has been observed paying more than 100฿ for TechSeed, because nobody has been asked.

**The job to be done, stated by both segments:** "certs to fill ports." Portfolio evidence for TCAS รอบ 1. Not "career exploration." Both SKUs serve the same job — one shallowly, one deeply — and the deep one is priced at 1/30th of the shallow one.

## Status Quo

What a Thai family does today to solve "my child needs portfolio evidence and a direction":

- Buys a one-day cert from whoever is offering one. Commodity market, low switching cost, no attachment.
- Hires an admissions consultant for the "already good" child. Entrenched, relationship-driven, expensive.
- Does nothing for the "hopeless" child, because nothing on the market addresses direction, only optimization.

BizSeed competes in lane one. TechSeed is the only thing PassionSeed has that addresses lane three, and it is priced as a snack.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

**Target:** the parent of a ม.4–ม.6 student who needs portfolio evidence before the TCAS Portfolio round, and who has already demonstrated willingness to pay for exactly this job (BizSeed proved 2,990฿ clears).

**Two segments, opposite products — do not merge them:**

| | "Already good" | "Hopeless" |
|---|---|---|
| Needs | which uni, which round, sharper portfolio | any direction at all |
| Product shape | short, expert, deadline-driven | weeks, bonds, transformation |
| Deadline | TCAS, hard date | none, so no urgency to buy |
| Proven WTP | high (2,990฿, BizSeed) | untested (one batch, 100฿) |
| Sticks around | no | yes |
| Competition | entrenched Thai admissions consultants | almost nobody |

**Narrowest wedge:** the next TechSeed batch, sold at a real price, with radar and plan included as bundled extras rather than as reasons to buy. No new code. Payment by PromptPay, by hand.

## Constraints

- No payment integration exists anywhere in the codebase (no Stripe, Omise, 2C2P, PromptPay). Manual collection works to roughly 50 seats; this is not a blocker at test volume.
- Radar content lives in Supabase (`radar_fields`, `radar_cards`, `radar_sources`), not the repo. Content changes are production DB writes.
- Small team. 326 commits in 90 days, roughly 456 file touches in hackathon paths versus 87 in radar. Hackathon is where the capacity actually goes.
- The access mission is real but unquantified: the founder has not decided whether near-free TechSeed is a commitment or an accident.
- "Designing life" (helping students pick uni and rounds) has been concierge-tested manually, but n is too small to read. It is an unvalidated expansion.

## Premises

1. **(Revised after pushback.)** TechSeed's multi-week cohort is the only thing tested commercially that produced both revenue and transformation. Radar, PathLab, and Plan are **untested, not disproven**. — AGREED, with the founder's correction incorporated.
2. Both segments are buying the same job: credible evidence for the TCAS portfolio. — AGREED, and expanded by the founder: the intent is to grow this into "designing life" (uni choice, round strategy), which is concierge-tested but n-too-small.
3. TechSeed's price is untested, not capped. One paid batch at 100฿ proves families will pay something; it says nothing about 1,000฿ or 1,900฿. — AGREED.
4. BizSeed's 2,990฿ proves the ceiling is high but the format is wrong. Same job, wrong delivery: one day buys a cert, weeks buy a transformation, and the cert costs more. — AGREED.
5. Radar's real job is demand capture for paid cohorts, not a standalone product. It already records per-career intent and drops to a LINE QR (`components/radar/RadarCards.tsx:1846`). That is a lead funnel being treated as content. — AGREED.
6. PathLab's real job is to cut cohort delivery cost, not replace the cohort. Async content does not produce bonds, and the bonds are the product. — AGREED.
7. Changed TechSeed alumni are the referral engine, and are currently unused as one. — AGREED.

**The premise the founder rejected, and was right to:** the original phrasing claimed radar/PathLab/Plan "produced neither revenue nor transformation," implying failure. They have never been tried commercially. Untested is the accurate word.

**The counter that still stands:** untested is the reason not to bet the company on them yet. The Phase 2 curriculum auto-fails a student team for a "decision made without any data." Shifting to radar + PathLab on the grounds that they are "easier to scale" is that same move. Easy-to-scale is a property of things nobody pays for — there is no delivery constraint because there is no delivery.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Price the thing that already works (minimal viable)
Run the next TechSeed batch at a real price, split-tested against the 100฿ baseline. Build nothing. Radar stays a free funnel; PathLab shelved for one cycle. PromptPay collection by hand.
- Effort: S (human: ~1 batch cycle / CC: near-zero, no code)
- Risk: Low
- Reuses: TechSeed program as-is, radar intent rows, LINE, existing mentors
- Pros: answers the single question blocking every downstream decision; costs no engineering; failure is cheap and informative
- Cons: costs one batch to learn; advances nothing on the scale story

### Approach B: Merge the two SKUs into one laddered product (ideal architecture)
BizSeed's price with TechSeed's format. Multi-week paid cohort ending in a portfolio artifact plus the cert, priced near BizSeed, run at Chula for legitimacy. Radar becomes the funnel. PathLab becomes the async spine cutting mentor cost per cohort. Alumni become the referral engine.
- Effort: M (human: ~1 quarter / CC: weeks of platform work)
- Risk: Med
- Reuses: everything — radar intent, PathLab, hackathon ops, Chula relationship, alumni
- Pros: every existing asset gets exactly one job; coherent company
- Cons: pricing it without A's data is guessing; drifts away from near-free access

### Approach C: Premium admissions funds free TechSeed (lateral)
Charge "already good" families for short, deadline-driven TCAS strategy. Use the margin to keep TechSeed near-free for "hopeless" kids.
- Effort: M (human: ~1 quarter / CC: minimal, mostly ops)
- Risk: Med-High
- Reuses: BizSeed customer list, concierge sessions, `/tcas` route
- Pros: protects the access mission explicitly; serves the segment with proven WTP
- Cons: two businesses with one small team; entrenched consultants own that market and PassionSeed has no brand there; the paying segment does not stick, so nothing compounds

## Recommended Approach

**Approach A, in the bundled form the founder chose: TechSeed + radar + plan, anchored around 1,000฿.**

The founder's selection: *"techseed,... maybe + radar's + plan's value priced aroud 1k."*

Rationale: 1,000฿ is 10x the tested baseline, which makes it a real test rather than a rounding error. Bundling radar and plan gives them a job without betting on them — they ride along as included value while the cohort carries the sale.

**Two conditions on this, both open:**

1. **Test above 1,000฿ in the same cycle.** BizSeed already cleared 2,990฿ for one day, no bonds, no transformation. Pricing weeks-with-bonds at 1/3 of that contradicts the founder's own data. Run at least one batch or one segment at ~1,900฿. If it clears, the business tripled at zero extra delivery cost. If it does not, 1,000฿ becomes proven rather than assumed.
2. **The cohort must be the anchor; radar and plan are included extras.** If radar is sold as the reason to buy, a sale will not reveal which component the family wanted, and a failure will not reveal which component killed it. The learning objective is "will a family pay ~1k for the cohort," and the bundle must not destroy it.

Approach B is the destination once A produces a number. Approach C is the fallback if A proves the ceiling is genuinely low.

## Open Questions

1. **Is near-free TechSeed a mission commitment or an unset price?** The founder answered "haven't tested." Until this is decided, it is unclear whether raising price is even permitted. If it is a commitment, Approach C becomes the primary path, not the fallback.
2. **What is the actual price ceiling?** 1,000฿ versus 1,900฿ versus 2,990฿. BizSeed argues the ceiling is high; only a test settles it.
3. **Which segment does "designing life" serve?** The concierge test has n too small to read. Running it further without separating "hopeless" from "already good" will keep producing unreadable results.
4. **What is TechSeed's delivery cost per student?** Without it, no price is knowable as profitable. Mentor hours per cohort is the number that matters.
5. **How many TechSeed alumni exist, and are any of them reachable?** The referral engine premise is untested and depends entirely on this.
6. **Does the cert survive at a higher price?** Both segments named the cert as the thing they are buying. Whether the cert or the transformation carries the 1k price is unknown.

## Success Criteria

The next batch settles this if:

- The batch is offered at a real price to a defined list, with at least two price points tested.
- Conversion is measured as *paid over asked*, not over reached — a number the team can state out loud.
- Cost per student is computed from actual mentor hours, so margin is known and not assumed.
- At least one price above 1,500฿ is put in front of real families, whatever the result.
- Whoever pays is asked, in one sentence, what they thought they were buying. Their answer beats the marketing copy.
- The decision that follows is Persevere / Pivot / Kill, referencing specific data points — the same bar Activity 4 sets for students.

Failure to hit the price ceiling is a valid and useful outcome. Failure to run the test is not.

## Distribution Plan

Existing deployment pipeline covers the web surface. The commercial distribution for this test needs no new infrastructure:

- **Demand capture:** radar intent rows already record interested-by-career and drop to a LINE QR.
- **Sales channel:** LINE, direct to the existing list, plus the online presence the founder is currently building.
- **Payment:** PromptPay QR, collected manually. Sufficient to roughly 50 seats.
- **Checkout integration:** build after somebody has paid, not before. Premature at this volume.

## Dependencies

- A defined list to sell to: TechSeed alumni, radar intent rows, hackathon participants.
- Mentor capacity for one batch at the new price.
- A decision on the access-mission question (Open Question 1) before any price above ~500฿ is quoted publicly.
- Nothing in the codebase blocks this. No feature work is a prerequisite.
