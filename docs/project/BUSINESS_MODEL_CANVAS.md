**Date:** 2026-06-26  
**Status:** Draft for validation  
**Source doc:** [`VALIDATION_MASTER.md`](VALIDATION_MASTER.md)

This Business Model Canvas is ready as a working version, not a final business truth. The problem and product wedge have meaningful early evidence. The revenue model is promising but still needs pricing and willingness-to-pay validation.

## 1. One-Line Business Thesis

PassionSeed is an Ikigai-based career planning membership that helps Thai high school students choose paths that are actually theirs, using career reality data, self-discovery, mentor context, and hands-on trials so they do not get pushed around by peer pressure, parent pressure, or low-quality social media.

## 3. Customer Segments

### Primary Users: High School Students

Initial focus:

- M3 students choosing high school track.
- M4-M6 students choosing faculty, major, portfolio direction, and early career path.
- High-achieving students with many options but low confidence.
- Creative/AI-curious students whose interests are not easily understood by parents.

Core student problem:

> "I have to choose my future, but I do not know what is real, what fits me, or what is just pressure from people around me."

### Economic Buyers: Parents

Parents likely pay because the decision has real financial and emotional consequences:

- Tuition.
- Tutoring.
- Portfolio preparation.
- Lost years from choosing the wrong path.
- Family conflict around "practical" vs "passion."

Parent problem:

> "I want my child to choose well, but I need evidence that their path is practical and not just a phase."

### Expansion Users: Alumni

Students who leave high school and enter university should remain in the PassionSeed ecosystem as alumni.

Alumni needs:

- Choosing internships.
- Building portfolio/projects.
- Finding mentors.
- Translating interests into career options.
- Navigating first job decisions.
- Giving back to younger students later.

### Later Institutional Buyers

Schools, counselors, and enrichment programs may become a channel or buyer after student/parent demand is clearer.

## 4. Value Propositions

### Core Value Proposition

PassionSeed helps students choose a path that belongs to them, not one forced by peer pressure, parent anxiety, or social-media noise.

### Student Value

- Know which careers and majors have real future potential.
- Understand the actual work behind attractive labels.
- Discover what they love, what they are good at, what the world needs, and what can pay.
- Try paths through small projects before committing years.
- Build confidence and language to explain their choices.
- Reduce regret from choosing based on hype, fear, or incomplete information.

### Parent Value

- See structured evidence behind the child's direction.
- Reduce the risk of wrong major, wrong track, and wasted tuition.
- Understand modern careers without relying only on old career categories.
- Support the child without losing sight of practicality.

### Alumni Value

- Continue from "choose a path" into "build the path."
- Get career exploration, project guidance, internship direction, and mentor access in college.
- Stay connected to a network of people pursuing meaningful work.
- Eventually become contributors, mentors, or success stories for younger members.

### Emotional Promise

> "You are not behind. You are learning how to choose with evidence, self-knowledge, and courage."

## 5. Ikigai as the Core Framework

Ikigai should be the operating system of PassionSeed, not a decorative concept.

| Ikigai Dimension | PassionSeed Product Expression | Main Question |
|---|---|---|
| What you love | Reflection, interest signals, energy/drain patterns | What pulls you in even when no one forces you? |
| What you are good at | Activities, project outputs, feedback, strengths | What kind of work do you naturally improve at? |
| What the world needs | Career Radar, market shifts, social/economic demand | Where is the world creating real demand? |
| What you can be paid for | Salary progression, entry requirements, career ladders | What can sustain your life, not just inspire you? |

The product should help students find overlap without pretending every passion instantly becomes a job. This is where trust matters: PassionSeed must be honest about tradeoffs, difficulty, money, and uncertainty.

## 6. Channels

### Evidence-Backed Early Channels

- Instagram/TikTok-style short content.
- Student referrals.
- Career Radar sharing.
- Seniors/mentors as trusted voices.
- Review/search behavior around majors and careers.

### Likely Conversion Channels

- Student shares Radar/report with parent through LINE.
- Parent sees report and buys yearly or 3-year membership.
- School workshop introduces students to Radar and Ikigai planning.
- Mentor/senior content drives trust into specific career paths.

### Channel Strategy

Start student-led, then convert parent-supported.

The student should feel like PassionSeed is theirs. The parent should feel like PassionSeed is credible enough to fund.

## 7. Customer Relationships

### Membership Relationship

PassionSeed should be positioned as a multi-year planning companion, not a one-time quiz.

Lifecycle:

1. Explore: student discovers possible paths.
2. Decide: student compares options and chooses near-term direction.
3. Test: student tries projects/PathLabs.
4. Plan: student creates roadmap for track, major, portfolio, or career.
5. Build: student creates proof through projects and activities.
6. Launch: student enters university/internship/early career.
7. Alumni: student stays connected, receives career support, and may mentor others.

### Parent Relationship

Parents receive clarity, not control.

Parent-facing product should show:

- What the student explored.
- Why the path fits or does not fit.
- What risks remain.
- What the next 3-6 months should look like.

### Alumni Relationship

The alumni layer should create long-term identity:

- "I grew through PassionSeed."
- "I still get support as I build my career."
- "I can help younger students avoid the confusion I had."

## 8. Revenue Streams

### Recommended Primary Model: Annual Membership

Annual subscription is strategically coherent because the decision journey is not one day. Students need repeated planning cycles across school years, admissions, portfolio building, and early career choices.

Possible membership promise:

> "One year of guided career and study-path planning."

Included:

- Career Radar access.
- Ikigai profile and updates.
- Path recommendations.
- Planning reports.
- Limited PathLab/project guides.
- Parent-shareable summaries.
- Alumni/community access while active.

### 3-Year High School Plan

The 3-year plan fits the high school journey especially well:

- M4 -> M6 faculty/portfolio planning.
- M3 -> M5 track/future exploration.
- Reduces annual renewal friction.
- Aligns with the family planning horizon.

Possible promise:

> "Support your child through the full high school decision journey."

### Lifetime Alumni Membership

Offer lifetime alumni membership to students who buy the 3-year plan.

This is strategically strong if alumni access is framed as identity and long-term career support, not unlimited expensive service.

Lifetime alumni can include:

- Alumni profile.
- Access to selected career resources.
- Community/events.
- Ability to mentor younger students later.
- Alumni-only career updates.
- Discounts for premium mentor sessions, workshops, or advanced programs.

Lifetime alumni should not include unlimited high-cost services like unlimited 1:1 mentorship unless priced accordingly.

### Pricing Hypotheses To Test

Do not commit yet. Test ranges:

| Product | Possible Price Test | Buyer |
|---|---:|---|
| Annual student membership | 999-2,990 THB/year | Parent/student |
| Premium annual membership with reports | 2,990-5,900 THB/year | Parent |
| 3-year high school plan | 5,900-12,900 THB one-time | Parent |
| PathLab/project sprint | 499-1,990 THB per sprint | Parent/student |
| Mentor/session add-on | 499-2,500 THB per session | Parent/student |

### Revenue Confidence

The yearly and 3-year model is a good strategic hypothesis. It is not validated until parents show payment behavior.

Next validation should ask for:

- PromptPay deposit.
- Paid pilot.
- 3-year waitlist.
- Parent phone number after seeing sample report.

## 14. Biggest Risks

| Risk | Why It Matters | Validation Test |
|---|---|---|
| Parents do not pay yearly | Revenue model fails. | Show report and ask for paid pilot/deposit. |
| Students use it once and leave | Subscription retention fails. | Track D1/D7/D30 return and second-path exploration. |
| Lifetime alumni becomes too costly | Gross margin gets damaged. | Limit lifetime benefits to low-marginal-cost access. |
| Ikigai feels too abstract | Students may not see practical value. | Tie every dimension to concrete Radar/PathLab outputs. |
| Career data loses trust | Product credibility collapses. | Source/date every claim; label levels clearly. |
| Parents hijack the decision | Student trust drops. | Design parent reports as support tools, not control panels. |

## 15. Validation Priorities

### Before Building Subscription Infrastructure

1. Create one sample paid report.
2. Show it to 15-25 parents.
3. Test annual vs 3-year framing.
4. Ask for payment, deposit, or waitlist commitment.
5. Measure whether students return after Radar.

### Specific Questions To Test

- Do parents prefer yearly payment or 3-year plan?
- Does "lifetime alumni member" increase willingness to buy the 3-year plan?
- What benefits do students expect from alumni membership?
- What benefits would be too expensive for PassionSeed to promise forever?
- Does Ikigai language resonate with students, parents, or both?
- Does "choose what is actually yours" outperform "choose a career with future ROI"?

We are ready to proceed with this Business Model Canvas v0.1:

- **Primary customer focus:** High school students.

- **Upsell model:** 3-year high school plan.

The next proof needed is not more strategy. It is payment behavior.
