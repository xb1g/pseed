## What we are actually doing

**Purpose:** guide the new generation to design their own future so they can live fully.

**The thesis, in one sentence:** the Thai admission system is pay-to-win and cram-and-forget,
but the TCAS Round 1 portfolio project is the single window in a student's entire education
where they are permitted to build something real — and we occupy that window and refuse to
let it be filled with a fake proof-of-concept.

We are not selling admissions help with education smuggled inside. The alignment between
what we sell (relief from admissions panic) and what we deliver (a real self-authored
project) is genuine.

**What we sell to parents:** their child ships a real project, with real users interviewed,
documented, in time to matter.

**What actually changes the student:** the project, and a community where building is normal.

## Where this came from

18 months. Four products. ~1,300 students. Traction, no growth.

| Product | Reach | Money | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| TechSeed | ~500 | 100฿, one paid batch, multi-week | Alumni changed. Bonds formed. Some came back to teach. |
| BizSeed | — | ~2,990฿, one day, onsite Chula | Cleared easily. Students don't stick around. |
| Hackathon | 800+ | free | Design thinking, students interviewed real users. Format works. |
| Passion Map | — | never priced | Untested commercially |
| Radar | — | never priced | Untested commercially |

**Diagnosis:** we built supply-side products in a demand-side market, and never captured
outcome data on any of it. The market told us what it values — 30x more money for 1/10th
the delivery time — and we kept building the thing nobody was asked to pay for.

**The growth blocker is delivery capacity, and the founder is the capacity.**

## The two discoveries that reshaped this

**1. Alumni come back to teach.** Students who arrived at zero returned as teachers.
Nobody asked them to. At least 5 have prior teaching experience from past TechSeed cohorts;
**Kyzz** asked directly to build TechSeed with us. This is unfakeable proof of trajectory
change AND the only capacity-scaling path that doesn't dilute what makes the program work.

**2. The gap isn't credentials — it's community.** Alumni who reach university stop
building. Absorbed by pub, games, uni activities, the next test. Not from lost interest —
from lost context. Nobody around them treats building as normal.

> "they lack communities for passion + project + values to keep them going and building
> something valuable to them"

The camp is the entry event. **The community is the product.** That is why there is no
calendar deadline: we are selling membership in the only room where this is normal.

## The offer

| | |
|---|---|
| **Name** | ProjectSeed — named for the artifact, not the audience. TechSeed / BizSeed become entry events. |
| **Price** | 2,990฿, single tier. Founding-batch promotional price for batch 1. |
| **Included** | Community, guidance, Radar, Passion Map, curriculum, group sessions. |
| **Paid mentorship tiers** | **None.** Removed deliberately — see below. |
| **Access** | 10% scholarship seats every batch. |
| **Guarantee** | The project ships, documented, with real users interviewed. **No admission guarantee.** No calendar deadline. |
| **Mentors** | Alumni (5+, incl. Kyzz). Not hiring. |
| **Marketing** | Publish student projects and alumni outcomes as a counter-rubric to pay-to-win competitions. |

**Why there are no paid mentorship tiers.** Selling mentorship separately broke the product
twice: richer students would buy better projects (reproducing the exact pay-to-win system we
exist to oppose), and the guarantee's critical input would have been an optional upsell
(meaning the base tier could not deliver what it promised). One price with guidance included
dissolves both.

**Why we guarantee the project and not admission.** We do not control the admission
committee, and by our own assessment that system is rigged. A shipped project is true
regardless of who reads it.

**Founding batch, not discount.** Founding = scarcity and status. Discount = everyone learns
the real price is the low one, forever. Ask every promo buyer what they thought they were
buying and what they would have paid — that converts a promo into price data.

### Batch 1 economics, plainly

```
20 seats − 2 scholarship (10%) = 18 paying
  × 1,990฿ founding promo → ~35,800฿ gross
  × 2,990฿ full price     → ~53,800฿ gross
```

Fine for a validation batch. Not a company yet. Whether it becomes one depends entirely on
alumni mentors becoming real capacity — which is unproven.

## Open risks — read these before you disagree with a decision

1. **Mentor-hours per student is unknown.** Flagged by two reviews, still unmeasured. Without
   it, no price is knowable as profitable. Measuring it in batch 1 is PS-193.
2. **Load accumulates, revenue does not.** No deadline means batch 1 students still consume
   guidance when batch 3 starts. Alumni supply is the answer; it is a bet.
3. **Volunteer mentor cliff.** Unpaid alumni typically disengage around month 4-6.
4. **Community-as-product is a hypothesis.** Based on the founder's observation of alumni who
   stayed in touch. Not yet validated.
5. **Alumni-as-capacity is a hypothesis.** 5 self-selected people are not a network, and
   teaching juniors is not the same skill as supervising ambiguous self-directed work.
6. **"Shipped" is undefined.** Without a quality bar we become the credential factory we
   oppose. PS-191.
7. **Safeguarding.** University-age mentors, minors, months of contact. Duty of care,
   separate from PDPA. PS-190 is a hard prerequisite to selling a seat.
8. **A recognizable ProjectSeed format may read as coached** to admissions readers.
9. **M4 vs M6 are different segments.** M4 has exploration time, M6 needs an artifact now.
   Deliberately deferred past batch 1.
10. **Nine failure modes have no defined response** — student never ships, refunds, promo
    price leakage, parent paid but student unwilling, mentor drops mid-batch, and others.

## Working rules

- **PDPA:** talk to past students conversationally through Discord / LINE OA channels they
  opted into. No formal survey. Written release — parental if under 20 — before publishing
  any student story or project, including Kyzz's.
- **Safeguarding:** no 1:1 private DMs with minors. Group channels, or a second adult present.
- **Evidence:** conversational outreach finds engaged survivors and misses quiet failures.
  Use it to find mentors and warm leads. Never to substantiate a published impact claim.
- **Payments:** manual PromptPay. Sufficient to ~50 seats. Build checkout after someone pays.
- **The binding constraint is execution, not planning.** Four approved strategy docs existed
  on this branch before a single customer conversation happened. Do not add a fifth.
